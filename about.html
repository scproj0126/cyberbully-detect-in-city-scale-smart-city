Project Background

This project focuses on cyberbullying identification and detection in social networks under smart-city contexts. As online communication becomes increasingly common in urban public spaces, community platforms, and service-feedback channels, aggressive language and cyberbullying can spread faster and amplify their social impact. Although text classification models have achieved strong results, city-scale deployment is often affected by cross-platform domain shift, class imbalance, and weak performance on minority bullying types, which can reduce stability and interpretability in real-world settings. To better align with smart-city governance scenarios, we propose a reproducible and multi-modal detection pipeline: centered on text semantic representations, fused with structured context signals (e.g., temporal features, interaction statistics, and thread-structure indicators); and, when available, incorporating a cross-platform link-context modality (e.g., Tweet–Vine mapping identifiers) to capture propagation and association patterns in a lightweight manner. Under a unified evaluation protocol, we will systematically compare classic machine learning methods with transformer baselines, and quantify the gains brought by “link context” via ablation studies, with particular attention to macro-level metrics and minority-class recall.

Through data-driven methods, we aim to model and analyze potential bullying signals in online text, providing references for follow-up research and system design.

Research Motivation

The motivation of this project is to narrow the gap between “course/benchmark tasks” and real smart-city deployment constraints. We focus not only on overall accuracy, but also on stable recognition of minority cyberbullying types under class imbalance and cross-domain shifts, as well as resource constraints and reproducibility requirements at city scale.

Therefore, we define “multi-modal” in an operational way as: text semantics + structured context + link-context signals. We quantify the contribution of structural social signals through ablation studies (text-only vs. text + link context), emphasizing macro-level metrics and minority-class recall to produce conclusions that better match practical governance needs and academic reproducibility practice.
